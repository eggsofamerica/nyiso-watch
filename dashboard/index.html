What triggers an alert

Thresholds set from 2023–2026 data so alerts come about once a week on average, in clusters during heat waves and cold snaps. Each alert gives the last time something comparable happened.

  • Price spikeAny zone's hourly average real-time LBMP above $500/MWh (5-minute blips that fade within the hour don't count). About 37 days in the past two years. Reports how many hours it lasted. Once per 18 hours unless the peak doubles.
  • Widespread negative pricesThree or more zones with a negative hourly average real-time LBMP in the same hour. About 6 days a year, mostly spring. Negative hours in Zone D alone are routine and don't count.
  • Day-ahead pricesA zone's day-ahead daily average above its 99th percentile for the past three years. Checked each morning for today (and tomorrow, if already posted).
  • Reserve shortage10-minute spinning reserve price averaging over $500/MWh for an hour. About 14 days in two years.
  • High demandNYCA load above 30,000 MW in summer or 24,000 MW in winter, with the last time it was this high.
  • Demand forecastNYISO's forecast for the next five days shows a peak above 29,000 MW (May–Sep) or 23,000 MW (Nov–Mar). A few days' warning before heat waves and cold snaps; repeats only if the forecast rises 3% or after three days.
  • RecordsAll-time and seasonal load, NYC load, lowest midday load, wind (hourly and daily), grid solar and other renewables, behind-the-meter solar, wind plus solar, and zero-carbon share since Indian Point closed.
  • Nuclear tripStatewide nuclear output falling more than 400 MW within 20 minutes.
  • EmergenciesA Major Emergency declaration, or emergency capacity requests to neighboring systems.
  • New binding constraintA transmission facility binding at a shadow price of $500 or more that hadn't bound at all in the prior two years.
  • Judgment callEach run also checks spreads, congestion and generation mix against their normal range for the month. Claude flags these only when they look new and meaningful.
